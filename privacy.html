Gym Bro privacy

Gym Bro is a personal training and food planner. This page explains what it stores and where.

On your phone

Your profile, logs, meals, weights and photos are stored only on your phone. Photos never leave it. You can back up and delete everything from the app.

On the Gym Bro server

  • Notifications: if you turn them on, your notification subscription, your reminder times and which of today's tasks are done, so reminders skip what you've already done.
  • Calendar: your next two weeks of sessions (dates, times, titles), so they can appear in your calendar.
  • Apple Health (optional): if you set up the Shortcut, daily steps, active calories, resting heart rate, weight and the times and titles of your next week's calendar events. Deleted after 35 days.

Food lookups

When you scan a barcode, the camera picture stays on your phone and only the barcode number is sent to Open Food Facts, a free food database, to find the calories. Searching for packaged food sends your search words there too.

Weather (optional)

If you set your town, Gym Bro keeps its location rounded to about 10 km and asks Open-Meteo, a free weather service, for the forecast. With notifications on, the rounded location is also stored on the Gym Bro server so reminders can mention rain or cold. Turn it off in the app and it's deleted at the next sync.

Google Calendar (optional)

If you connect Google Calendar, Gym Bro asks only for permission to create and manage its own "Gym Bro" calendar. It can't see or change your other calendars or events. It writes your sessions into that calendar and reads back changes you make to them there. The access token is stored on the Gym Bro server. Disconnecting in the app deletes the Gym Bro calendar and revokes access; you can also remove access at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

Gym Bro's use of information received from Google APIs follows the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Data is never sold, shared with anyone else, or used for advertising.
